Damages · who publishes what, and when

After a climate event, when will anyone credible say what it cost?

For most of the world, the honest answer is never.

After a climate event, when will a credible damage figure exist, and who will publish it? Each row is one hazard in one territory. The axis is days after the event. A mark is a point at which some named body publishes a figure, and an empty row is a place where nobody has committed to publishing anything at all.

Filled a dated commitment the estimator publishes itself · open inferred from repeated behaviour, not promised · ringed happens only if triggered. The heavy tick is where a figure stops moving. This is a schedule basis, not the evidence basis used elsewhere on the site.

Europe's largest fire anomaly of 2026 is occurring in the one peril and territory combination where no public loss estimator exists. There may never be a published insured-loss figure for the France and Spain fires.

Row by row, and what each silence means

European windstorm

Settles: about 12 months

The densest early coverage of any hazard and territory in this map, because PERILS commits to dated restatements.

watched, and below the publishing threshold PERILS publishes nothing below EUR 300 million in a single country or EUR 500 million pan-European. The threshold is published and the estimator was watching, so silence here is weak evidence the event fell below it.

US wildfire, hurricane and severe convective storm

Settles: sigma in the Q1 following the event year

The fastest figures here are the least documented. AccuWeather publishes within a day with no methodology; the documented estimators take months.

watched, and below the publishing threshold PCS resurveys only above USD 250 million, and the billion-dollar series is billion-dollar by definition. Silence is weak evidence the event fell below those thresholds, not evidence of nothing.

Australian bushfire and cyclone

Settles: about 12 months

PERILS covers Australia, so the schedule is as dated as Europe's.

watched, and below the publishing threshold PERILS covers both the peril and the territory here. Its Australian reporting threshold is not verified in this map, so treat silence as unexplained until it is.

Indonesian fire and haze

Settles: no settled figure expected within the event year; the 2015 season was still being re-estimated in 2021

No insured-loss index covers Indonesian wildfire. The authoritative figures arrive years later and disagree; see the 2015 decomposition.

not covered by any estimator No insured-loss index covers Indonesian wildfire. Silence says nothing whatsoever about the size of the event.

Southern Africa drought

Settles: often never, as a money figure

Slow-onset drought has no event end date, so no schedule anchored on one can apply. The humanitarian system produces caseloads and appeals rather than loss estimates. This is the hardest hazard in the map and the honest answer is that a damage figure may never arrive.

not covered by any estimator No estimator produces damage figures for slow-onset drought here. The humanitarian system produces appeals, which measure response scale rather than loss. Silence says nothing about size.

Coastal Peru and Ecuador flooding

Settles: years; the 1982-83 event is still cited from EM-DAT in 2017 dollars

GRADE is the fastest credible option here and it is conditional on being requested, so its absence is not evidence of a small event.

not covered by any estimator GRADE is produced on request rather than automatically, so its absence is not evidence of a small event.

Mediterranean wildfire (France, Spain, Portugal, Greece)

Settles: no scheduled settlement; the quarterly recaps are the only dated commitments

The deepest coverage hole in this map, and deeper than first recorded. THREE separate mechanisms exclude it. PERILS covers neither wildfire as a European peril nor Spain, Portugal and Greece as territories. Spain's Consorcio de Compensacion de Seguros does NOT class wildfire as an extraordinary risk, so it publishes no wildfire loss figure; cover sits with private household insurers, with only narrow Consorcio involvement for personal injury during firefighting under Order PCM/641/2021. France's CatNat scheme, reinsured by CCR, EXCLUDES forest fire under the law of 13 July 1982; building damage from a forest fire is met by ordinary fire cover, not CatNat. So no public estimator produces an insured-loss figure for this hazard and territory at all.

not covered by any estimator No estimator covers this hazard and territory: excluded by PERILS on both peril and territory, by the Consorcio as not an extraordinary risk, and by the French CatNat scheme by statute. Silence says nothing whatsoever about size.
