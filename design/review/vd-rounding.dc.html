The Long Swell Rounding · ruling and one trap From design

Round the count. Never divide by the rounded number.

Kristjan's ruling: round where it helps a reader, and put the precise figure elsewhere on the page. Your card already does this and your note explaining it is right. Applying it to Paris is safe. Applying it as a rule across 21 cities is not, and the reason is worth having before the template is built.

The trap

Paris rounds 1.80 UP to two, so a ratio taken from the rounded number gets SMALLER, 15 times rather than 16.7. That is the direction we want and it is why your card note is correct. But most baselines round down, and then the same move inflates the claim.

Marseille is the worst case: 23.7 times becomes 34. Rounding the baseline would overstate the ratio in 9 of the 21 cities, and it is conservative in 12. A rule that flatters us in nine cities out of twenty-one is not a rounding convention, it is a bias with a rounding convention in front of it.

The rule that works

Round what you DISPLAY. Compute from what you MEASURED. The rounded number is a label; it is never an input.

Your page already satisfies this and I would not change it. It shows two squares against thirty and states no ratio at all. The reader does the division by looking, which is exact, and no rounded figure is ever multiplied by anything. That is the design solving the problem rather than a caveat solving it.

So the guidance for the other twenty cities: keep the two counts and keep refusing the ratio. If a ratio ever has to be quoted, take it from the unrounded baseline and round the RESULT, and round that result down. Three cities already publish a count with no multiple for a related reason, so the page shape does not need one.
