`heat_city_series.json` is in this project. 21 cities, each with days_by_year, nights_by_year and warmest_night_c as {year, count} arrays, every one counted to that city's own cut. Paris has 78 day-years and 78 night-years. Your panels should fill.

The correction

Your page pairs 38 with 5.7, labelled "by this date in an average summer". 5.7 is a whole-year mean. The matched to-date figure is 3.73.

Paris, days above 30.2 C, its own hottest tenth

  2026, to 3 August              38
  1961-1990, TO 3 AUGUST        3.73   <- use this
  1961-1990, whole year         5.73   <- the payload's b6190, what 5.7 is

Your own side-note is what catches it: "Both rows stop on 3 August. A part-finished 2026 is never set against a complete season, and neither row is a summer total." The note is right; the number is not matched to it.

The error understates you. 38 against 3.73 is 10.2 times. 38 against 5.7 reads as 6.6. Pairing a part-season count with a whole-year baseline always understates, because the whole-year baseline is bigger. My own headline made the identical mistake this morning and product caught it; this is the third instance today, which is why it is worth naming as a class rather than an error.

The card
Reads “used to get about four hot days by now”, not six.
The row label
1961-1990, not “the nineteen seventies and eighties”. The window starts in 1961.
Keep the 90th
You are on the 90th percentile, the hottest tenth, which is the better choice for a reader. Heat RANKS on the 95th, so do not put a rank beside a 90th-percentile count: they are different measures. Your “No rank here” note already protects the series panel. The nights claim, more than any year Paris has recorded, is a nights rank and is correct.
What not to lose

The three notes in your right-hand column are the best writing on the page and should survive any revision. Not mirrored, because a hot night is not the opposite of a hot day, which is a better reason than the one design had for mirroring. Not stacked, because the total is not a quantity anyone can name. The ratio refused on nights, because dividing by about one a year produces a big number and no evidence. That last one states a gate in a reader's language and is the thing the calibration work exists for.
