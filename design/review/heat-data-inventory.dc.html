Heat has two instruments, not one

Generated from the payload, so every number here is live rather than described. Inventory, not a proposal. This replaces the earlier version: ECA&D has been deleted from the pipeline rather than demoted, so the warning about never opening the cross-check file is gone with it. All fifteen cities now sit on published commercial sources, with nights and days from the same rows of the same record.

The finding to design around

Two independent instruments, and they agree almost everywhere. That is the finding, and it is harder to dismiss than a disagreement would be.

Nights are counted against a station record. Days are counted above that station's own 95th percentile of July-August maxima, 1971-2000. Different thresholds, different times of day, same rows of the same record. 7 of the fifteen cities are at an outright record on BOTH. Not one is extreme on one instrument and ordinary on the other.

Where they part company they part by DEGREE, never by direction. The widest gap is Marseille at 7.8 percentile points, 7th of 77 on nights against 1st of 77 on days. That is not nights being wrong: 7 of 77 is the 91st percentile, and the channel's own lead says no city here is having an ordinary summer. It is one reading running further than the other.

The design question, from Kristjan. How does a reader see whether something is abnormal when there are two readings? One abnormal and one not is a weaker statement; both abnormal is a stronger one. That needs a visual grammar rather than a sentence, and the page cannot lead on a single number: Madrid is 1st on nights and 3rd on days, Seville 3rd and 8th, Marseille 7th and 1st.

THE CAVEAT INVERTS BETWEEN THE INSTRUMENTS
Nights · may NOT say

2026 is NOT the worst year on this measure. 2003 produced 12 on the same to-date basis and is inside living memory for this readership.

Days · MAY say

2026 sets more city day-records than any year in the window, including 2003. This is the OPPOSITE of the nights constraint, where 2003 remains worse and `may_not_say` applies. The two instruments do not share a caveat.

Same channel, same year, opposite permissions. Carrying the nights caveat onto days would understate a true finding; carrying the days claim back to nights would be false. Both prohibitions travel as payload fields rather than as house style, so a renderer cannot get this wrong quietly. If any single thing on this page argues that the nuance IS the product, it is this pair.

The band is the same width for every city and that is correct. The window is fixed at 1991-2020, so n is 30 everywhere and Madrid's 106-year record buys it no extra precision. Heat's ruling, and the reason is better than standardisation: a full-record sd would span a century of a warming climate and so measure trend as well as spread, inflating sd and understating z. The quantity wanted is the spread of the CURRENT climate. So a longer record does buy precision on most things and not on this one.

The two files
heat/data/city_nights.json
The current reading per city: nights, rank, day counts and thresholds, sources, and the headline with its baseline. This is what a page renders from.
heat/data/city_series.json
The histories. Per city per year: window_days, full_days, usability flags, nights to the cut, nights full year, and day counts. This is what a chart draws from, and where the gaps are.

What a renderer may not do
tie_rule
A TIE IS NOT A RECORD. rank = 1 + the number of prior years whose count is greater than OR EQUAL TO the current year. Three cities tie 2026, so recomputing a rank with a strict greater-than would manufacture a record. Stated as a field because a convention that lives only in the code gets re-derived differently.
rank, never derived
Take rank from the payload. Recomputing it with a strict greater-than promotes ties to first place and would manufacture a record, taking the headline from 8 of 15 to 9 of 15.
requires_series
This rank may not be rendered without the series below it. A bare rank is an alarm; the same rank beside its ordinary years is a calibrated statement.
headline_requires_baseline
The count of 8 may not appear without its baseline: a typical year produces 0, and with no trend the expected number is 0.19.
may_not_say
2026 is NOT the worst year on this measure. 2003 produced 12 on the same to-date basis and is inside living memory for this readership.
counted_to vs last_observation
Two separate fields. Sources now reach one day past the cut, and the cut is frozen through the launch because advancing it moves no rank but changes fourteen counts. The difference is visible rather than implied.
cities_without_day_multiple_note
These stations opened after 1961, so their 1961-1990 baseline is part-length and drawn from the warmer end of the period. The multiple is NOT EMITTED rather than flagged, so it cannot be rendered or reinstated. Their recent rate stands on a complete window and is emitted.
the day threshold is not ours
AEMET's own published rule. Verified by reproducing their published Madrid 36.4 C and Seville 41.2 C exactly. There is no Meteo-France equivalent, so the French thresholds are the same method with weaker verification, and that sentence must not be copied across.
Gaps are real, and Barcelona is the case

Paris no longer has a gap: the 22-year hole was an ECA&D artefact and Meteo-France has continuous coverage. Barcelona's are genuine, three present-runs at 1924-1927, 1938 alone, and 1944-2025. They are the Civil War and the war years, so the absence means something, which makes it a better case than Paris ever was. Completeness bar is 0.9 over the window 1 May to each city's own cut.

Why the lead is not the record count

“Not one of these cities is having an ordinary summer for hot nights.”

15 of 15 are in the warmest tenth of their own record and 14 in the warmest twentieth. The record count can move on a data update, since Malaga leads by one night; this framing cannot. Three of the four worst years on this measure are the last four years. A pattern rather than an event.

What is NOT in the payload

No standard deviation, so no z: rank and percentile only. No sub-annual detail, so no daily or monthly values, only annual counts. No cities outside Spain and France, and the metric does not travel north. No projection and no forecast. No population or exposure figures, so nothing here supports a harm claim.
