Design · mockups for reaction, not for publishing

Two fast-reaction pieces, and the decisions inside them

Built on real numbers so the shapes are honest. Where a choice is open this shows both options and says which way I lean, rather than picking quietly. Piece 1 is entirely our own validated data. Piece 2 is not ours at all and carries that on its face. Neither chart invents a data point: where we do not hold a series, the space says so.

Decision 1 · how the page says it measures rainfall, not flooding

A reader arrives at a flood page with flooding in mind and will supply the word we did not. Harder than the crops instrument problem, because the channel name and the reader's expectation both point the wrong way.

Option A · in the claim itself

69.4 mm of RAIN fell on the Eastern Pyrenees in a fortnight, the most in 27 years.

The word is load-bearing and unmissable. Costs the sentence some grace.

Option B · claim clean, instrument line directly under it

The Eastern Pyrenees had their wettest fortnight in 27 years.

Measures rainfall · not flood extent

Reads better and puts the qualifier one line below the claim rather than inside it.

I lean A. D-051 says a qualifier travels with its datum, and on this page the qualifier IS the finding's boundary. B is one scroll from being screenshotted without the tag.

Decision 2 · flood extent is not assessed, and that is a finding

D-193: the screen passed 0 of 6 European regions, not because Europe cannot be seen but because week-to-week visibility varies enough that a ranking would rank the weather over the sensor.

Option A · a second instrument row, deliberately empty

Rainfall69.4 mm1st of 27
Flood extentnot assessed
no flood-extent baseline has been built for this region, so the instrument's ability to see it is unknown and untested

The gap is drawn at the same weight as the measurement, so absence cannot read as zero.

Option B · prose only, under “what this is not”

We have not measured flooding here; this page reports rainfall only. Flood extent is not assessed for European regions because satellite visibility varies week to week.

Honest, and it sits below the fold where the reader has already formed a view.

I lean A, strongly. This project's recurring defect is absent read as zero, and a row that exists and says "not assessed" is the only version a skimming reader cannot miss.

13 of 14 days compared. 08-10 absent from the source and excluded from EVERY year, not just this one, so the comparison stays like for like. Mockup 2 · heat · Lima

Corrected since you last saw thisThis piece originally said the five warmest August nights ARE the El Nino list, in order. That was false and heat caught it against our own ONI: August 1983 was NEUTRAL, ONI −0.24, sitting in the decay of the 1982-83 event. The true claim is FOUR of the five. Weaker, and it survives contact with our own year-status file, which the stronger one did not. The figures are also ours now: heat emitted heat/data/lima_nights.json and this reads from it rather than from a message.

Lima · winter 2026 · GHCN record, current winter from station bulletins

Lima has had 75 of its last 77 winter nights at or above 20 °C.

June 29 of 29, July 30 of 30, August 16 of 18. The warmest, 21.7 °C on 14 August, is above anything in the station’s August record.

The chart, and it is the whole piece

The five warmest August nights in Lima’s GHCN record, and 2026. Four of the five fell in an El Nino August, marked below; 1983 did not, and is not marked. Each August is labelled by the ocean AT THAT AUGUST, not by its calendar year. No European page of ours can show this.

Six points, not a series: the top five and this year. Drawn as six discrete bars so it cannot be read as a complete history. Heat has since emitted the full record, so the option below is now possible and was not when this was first drawn.

Option · the whole record, now that it exists

All 36 measured Augusts in GHCN, El Nino Augusts marked, 2026 at the right. It makes the marking argument better rather than worse: on six bars a reader takes our word for the pattern, and here they can see it, including the 9 El Nino Augusts that were not warm.

Offered, not substituted. You have ruled on the chart; this only became possible when heat emitted the record.

Decision 4 · count versus peak, and it is our distinction

Option A · lead with the count

75 of Lima’s last 77 winter nights stayed at or above 20 °C.

The viral framing is one record night. Ours is persistence, which is the same argument that made Lugano work.

Option B · lead with the record night, count as support

Lima’s warmest August night on record, above 1997.

Sharper, more shareable, and it is the framing everyone else will already have used.

I lean A and it is not close. B is a race we lose on speed and win on nothing; A is a claim only a baseline can make, and the chart underneath it does the shareable work anyway.

Decision 5 · SETTLED: mark the years, claim nothing further

Kristjan's call. The chart marks which Augusts were El Nino and the prose does not argue from it. The reader connects; we do not. That posture is the reason the correction above cost us a word and not a page: nothing in the copy had been built on five of five, because the copy never made the claim.

Worth noting what nearly happened. The stronger claim was the one everybody wanted, including me, and it was wrong in the one direction that would have embarrassed the thesis it was meant to support: a La Nina year among the five warmest, on a chart arguing warm nights are El Nino nights.
