## headline

How a flood is made

## standfirst

Barcelona's wettest day since its complete record began in 1978 came when
three things lined up: a sea after its warmest September in 87 years, a
pool of cold Atlantic air, and a wind driving moist air onshore.

## bath-1

On 3 October the Fabra observatory in Barcelona measured **159.1 mm** of
rain. One millimetre of rain is one litre on every square metre.

## bath-2

So a one-metre square of ground at the gauge took 159 litres in a day.
For scale only: a full bath holds around 150.

## bath-3

That is the wettest day at Fabra in its complete record, which starts in
1978.

## bath-4

Rain like that needs three things at once: warm water to feed it, a way to
lift it, and something to set it off. Here is how they lined up.

## heat

September was warm across Europe, but the sea was the outlier. The western
and central Mediterranean had their **warmest September in a record going
back to 1940**: 2.4 °C and 2.8 °C above the 1991 to 2020 normal.

## sea

The western Mediterranean averaged **26.0 °C** in September. Normal is
23.6. That is the record. Rain on this coast starts as sea water, lifted
into the air.

## vapour

The air held more water too. Across the whole planet, not just Spain,
September 2026 carried **more water vapour than any September in a record
going back to 1940**, 6.5% above normal. Warmer air holds about 7% more
water for each degree.

## pump-1

27 September. Five kilometres up, a pool of unusually cold air is sitting
over the Atlantic.

## pump-2

29 September. At its deepest it is **15.9 °C colder than normal** for the
date, far out over the ocean.

## pump-3

30 September. It swings toward Iberia, still 11.7 °C colder than normal,
out in the Atlantic north-west of Galicia.

## pump-4

1 October. It weakens and breaks up, leaving a shallow dip in the air over
Spain.

## pump-5

3 October. A kilometre and a half up, the wind off Barcelona blows in from
the south-east at 10 metres a second, **off a sea that had just had its
warmest September in 87 years.**

## pump-wk-1

18 October. In ECMWF's forecast, a new cold pool drops over France, about
10 °C colder than normal. Unlike the first storm, its edge reaches over
Spain, around 4 °C colder.

## pump-wk-2

19 October. In the forecast, the wind a kilometre and a half up turns
onshore over the warm sea: from the east at 14 metres a second off
Valencia, from the south-east at 11 off Barcelona.

## ground

The ground is the part still in place. In the second week of October,
topsoil from the Ebro delta to Murcia was wetter than normal for the date.
Around the Ebro delta it was **55% above normal, the second wettest in 36
years.** Ibiza was the exception, drier than usual. Ground that is already
wet takes in less of the next rain.

## weekend

The same ingredients are lining up again in ECMWF's forecast for this
weekend: first the cold air and the wind, as the European centre's model
sees them. It is their forecast, not ours. Then the map: where floods have
hit this coast before, how wet the ground is now, and AEMET's warnings as
they are issued.

## not

This is not an El Niño story. There is no reliable link between El Niño
and autumn rain in Europe.
