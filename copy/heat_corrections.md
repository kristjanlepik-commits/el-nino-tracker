# Heat city corrections

EDITOR OWNS THIS FILE. Design renders it and writes none of it.

One `## <city>` block per correction. The block is rendered above the
chart on that city's page, and the build REFUSES to publish a city whose
published record claim has been withdrawn and which has no block here
(`check_no_silent_claim_reversal` in design/make_city_pages.py). So a
correction cannot ship silently, and a block cannot be forgotten.

## THE RULE, ratified by product on editor's proposal, 2026-08-12

**A correction explains the error and never restates the current value.**
The value lives in the assembled row beside it and moves with the data.

Palma is why. The first block said "2026 ties rather than leads" and "the
rank reads second of 49", both of which were restatements of the current
value, and both went stale within a day: the bar rose on 11 August, then
Spain refreshed to 08-08 and broke the tie. A block that names a rank has
to be rewritten every time the rank moves, and the day it is not, the page
carries a correction that is itself wrong.

**And only a mistake is a correction.** The bar rising was an error we
made. The cut advancing was not; it is the data being current, and a
correction block that explains a refresh is a correction block explaining
that we published on time.

Delete a block only when the correction has stopped being news to a
reader returning to the page. It is not an errata log; it is the page
explaining itself.

## palma

Until 11 August this page called 2026 Palma's most, measured against a bar
this station could not support: our threshold uses a city's own July and
August temperatures from 1971 to 2000, and Palma's record starts in 1978,
so seven of those thirty years do not exist here. A part-length window
drawn from the cooler end set the bar too low. Palma now uses the complete
1991 to 2020 normal, and the bar rises from 33.0 °C to 33.6 °C.

**What changed is the bar, not any temperature we had already recorded.**
