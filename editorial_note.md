**Record ECMWF, and a new rung at +3.5°C.** The July ECMWF forecast came in at its hottest yet: a median near +4°C in the central Pacific, with essentially every ensemble member above +3.5°C. The headline probabilities below barely moved, and that is by design; our number blends six forecast models, and one hot run cannot drag the consensus far on its own. With the strong-to-super picture now near-certain, the live question has shifted to how far beyond the record this event could go. So the ladder gains a sixth rung: the chance of a peak above +3.5°C, roughly a full degree beyond the 1997/2015 record, currently reading around two in five.

Treat that number with the most caution of anything on the page. No official agency forecasts a threshold that extreme; it rests almost entirely on the dynamical models and leans hardest on that single record-hot ECMWF run. Read it as where the hot models are clustering, not a calibrated probability. If the models cool, it moves first and furthest. The observations, meanwhile, keep climbing into the forecast: weekly Niño 3.4 is around +1.7°C and westerly wind bursts are still firing.
