How a flood is made

Barcelona's wettest day since its complete record began in 1978 came when three things lined up: a sea after its warmest September in 87 years, a pool of cold Atlantic air, and a wind driving moist air onshore.

By Kristjan Lepik

01

Rain

How much is 159 millimetres?

On 3 October the Fabra observatory in Barcelona measured 159.1 mm of rain. One millimetre of rain is one litre on every square metre.

So a one-metre square of ground at the gauge took 159 litres in a day. For scale only: a full bath holds around 150.

That is the wettest day at Fabra in its complete record, which starts in 1978.

Rain like that needs three things at once: warm water to feed it, a way to lift it, and something to set it off. Here is how they lined up.

02

Heat

How hot did the summer get?

September was warm across Europe, but the sea was the outlier. The western and central Mediterranean had their warmest September in a record going back to 1940: 2.4 °C and 2.8 °C above the 1991 to 2020 normal.

03

Sea

Where did the water come from?

The western Mediterranean averaged 26.0 °C in September. Normal is 23.6. That is the record. Rain on this coast starts as sea water, lifted into the air.

Temperatures measured: western Mediterranean (Alboran, Balearic, Gulf of Lion), September mean, ERA5; 2026 is the warmest September in a record going back to 1940 (1 of 87). The motion is illustrative: the warmer sea is drawn with a heat shimmer and slightly denser vapour, not to a measured rate.

04

Air

How much water can air hold?

The air held more water too. Across the whole planet, not just Spain, September 2026 carried more water vapour than any September in a record going back to 1940, 6.5% above normal. Warmer air holds about 7% more water for each degree.

Global total column water vapour, area-weighted, ERA5. About 7% more water vapour per degree C of warming (Clausius-Clapeyron; IPCC AR6 WG1 ch. 8).

27 September. Five kilometres up, a pool of unusually cold air is sitting over the Atlantic.

29 September. At its deepest it is 15.9 °C colder than normal for the date, far out over the ocean.

30 September. It swings toward Iberia, still 11.7 °C colder than normal, out in the Atlantic north-west of Galicia.

1 October. It weakens and breaks up, leaving a shallow dip in the air over Spain.

3 October. A kilometre and a half up, the wind off Barcelona blows in from the south-east at 10 metres a second, off a sea that had just had its warmest September in 87 years.

06

Ground

Why does wet ground matter?

The ground is the part still in place. In the second week of October, topsoil from the Ebro delta to Murcia was wetter than normal for the date. Around the Ebro delta it was 55% above normal, the second wettest in 36 years. Ibiza was the exception, drier than usual. Ground that is already wet takes in less of the next rain.
