The Long Swell HeatWeek of 3 August 2026

How hot has the European summer been?

Twenty-four cities, each measured against its own thermometer and its own record rather than against each other. A hot day is one at or above that city's own 95th percentile for July and August, so 41.2 °C in Seville and 31.4 °C in Berlin. 14 of the 24 have had more of them than in any year on record. In a typical year 0 of them do.

How far from normal, city by city

Ordered by how many of that city's own recorded summers this one beats. Alicante has had 21 hot days, more than in any of its 88 recorded summers; Stockholm has had 6, which beats 76 in 100 of its own. 14 of the 24 beat every summer they have on record, so they tie at the top and are listed alphabetically inside that tie: nothing separates them, and inventing an order would mean ranking by how long each station has been running. Each strip is that city's whole record on one shared scale, so rows compare and a crowded strip is simply a longer record.

Why we publish two measurements and not one
MarseilleIts most hot days on record, and 7th of 77 for hot nights. The days run further than the nights.
BerlinHot days above 87 per cent of its own summers, and hot nights at the 71st percentile. Here it is the other way round.

If one measurement stood in for the other, those two cities would lean the same way. They lean opposite ways, which is how you can tell the instruments measure different things. 10 cities publish no night ratio at all, each averaging under two hot nights a year: dividing by a base that thin produces a large number and no evidence. The gate is a flag on each city rather than a threshold applied here.
