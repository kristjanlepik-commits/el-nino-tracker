The Long Swell HeatWeek of 3 August 2026

How hot has the European summer been?

Twenty-one cities, each measured against its own thermometer and its own record rather than against each other. A hot day is one at or above that city's own 95th percentile for July and August, so 41.2 °C in Seville and 31.4 °C in Berlin. 14 of the 21 have had more of them than in any year on record. A typical year produces 0.

How far from normal, city by city

Ordered by how far this summer sits from that city's own 1991-2020 normal, measured against how much its summers normally vary. Nice has had 34 hot days where 4.0 is normal for it; Berlin has had 8 where 5.7 is normal. That is the question a reader is asking, and it is not the same as which city broke the longest record: a long record makes a rank rarer without making the summer hotter. Each strip is every summer that station has recorded, one tick each, with 2026 in blue. The strip is scaled to that city alone. Bars are not comparable between cities: every one is on its own threshold and its own history.

Why we publish two measurements and not one
MarseilleIts most hot days on record, and 7th of 77 for hot nights. The days run further than the nights.
BerlinHot days above 87 per cent of its own summers, and hot nights at the 71th percentile. Here it is the other way round.

If one measurement stood in for the other, those two cities would lean the same way. They lean opposite ways, which is how you can tell the instruments measure different things. 7 cities publish no night ratio at all, each averaging under two hot nights a year: dividing by a base that thin produces a large number and no evidence. The gate is a flag on each city rather than a threshold applied here.
