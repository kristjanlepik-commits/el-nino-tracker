Spain, France, and the UK are each at their highest fire week in the 15-year satellite record

VIIRS satellite fire detections for the calendar week Jul 20-26, in every year of the single-sensor record (SNPP, 2012-2026). Each country is compared only to itself; the dashed line is its own 14-year mean for this week of the year. Spain and the UK have only had their night satellite pass today, so their 2026 bars are hatched: those totals are floors that will rise this evening.

What a detection is. One detection is one ~375 m satellite pixel observed actively burning on one overpass. A large fire front counts as many pixels at once, and a fire that keeps burning is counted again on every twice-daily pass it stays hot, so weekly counts measure extent and persistence together, not the number of fires. Detections are not burned area and not severity.

Method. NASA FIRMS, VIIRS on Suomi-NPP only for cross-year consistency, low-confidence detections excluded, points assigned to countries by national boundary polygons applied identically in all years. 2026 comes from the near-real-time product; 2012-2025 from the science-quality archive, which typically revises NRT counts slightly when it catches up.

Context. France's week is dominated by the Gironde and Landes complex, which peaked on Jul 24. Spain's 2022 comparison bar is the Sierra de la Culebra summer. UK counts are small in absolute terms; its record is relative to its own low base. Southern Europe's fire season is driven by heat and drought in the Mediterranean, which this tracker treats as a non-El Nino control region: these fires are not presented as an El Nino signal.
