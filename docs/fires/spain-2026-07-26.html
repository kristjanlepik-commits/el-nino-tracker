Only today's night satellite pass has been processed so far. The afternoon pass, which usually carries most of a day's detections, is expected around 16:30 UTC. Today's bar and the week total are therefore floors, not totals.

Unlike France, Spain shows no sign of a peak. Its daily counts have climbed for three consecutive days, and today's partial figure already exceeds every complete day of the week so far.

What a detection is. One detection is one satellite pixel, about 375 m across, seen radiating enough heat to be flagged as actively burning during a single overpass. A large fire front registers as many pixels at once, and a fire that keeps burning is counted again on every twice-daily pass it stays hot, so a weekly count measures how much ground was visibly alight and for how long. It is not a count of separate fires, and it is not burned area.

Method. NASA FIRMS active fire data from the VIIRS instrument on the Suomi-NPP satellite, used alone so that every year in the chart is measured by the same sensor. Low-confidence detections are excluded, and detections are assigned to countries using national boundary polygons applied identically in all years. The 2026 figures come from the near-real-time product; 2012 to 2025 come from the science-quality archive, which usually revises near-real-time counts slightly when it catches up. Heavy smoke and cloud suppress detections, so the worst fire days can be undercounted.

Attribution. Southern and western European fires are driven by heat, drought and wind, and people supply nearly all ignitions. This tracker treats the Mediterranean as a non-El Nino control region and does not present these fires as an El Nino signal.
