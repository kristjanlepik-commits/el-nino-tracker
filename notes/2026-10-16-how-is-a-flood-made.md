# How is a flood made?

I built a visual explainer to find out. It starts weeks before it rains.

This summer the seas around Europe have been warmer than usual. The western Mediterranean had its warmest September in 87 years of records: 26.0 °C, against a normal of 23.6.

But the warm sea alone does not create a flood, it is a precondition. You also need a pool of cold air moving in high up, which is why these floods come in autumn, and wind to push the wet air onto the coast.

This already happened two weeks ago. Barcelona had its wettest day since its complete record began in 1978: 159 litres of water on every square metre at the Fabra observatory. That is a full bathtub of water for one square metre! (I had to double check.)

To me this shows that everything in nature is one system: the heat and the floods are the same system showing different results.

For this weekend the European weather centre (ECMWF) has a similarly worrying forecast for Spain and other parts of Europe, so I added that to the explainer too.
