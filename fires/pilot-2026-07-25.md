# Fire tracker: pilot issue

**PILOT.** Assembled Saturday 2026-07-25 with real data, as a format
test. This is not the first official issue; that remains Monday
2026-08-03 per the spec. Analog-year same-week comparisons are live
(FIRMS archive via MAP_KEY, obtained during assembly); the full
2012-2025 climatology strips arrive with `fire_baselines.md`.

Activity data: NASA FIRMS near-real-time, VIIRS SNPP + NOAA-20
combined, low-confidence detections excluded, complete satellite days
2026-07-18 through 2026-07-24 (UTC). Detections are counts of 375 m
fire pixels, not fires and not burned area.

## Lede

The northern-hemisphere fire season is running hot before the El Nino
windows even open: the US is at Preparedness Level 5 with burned area
at 123% of its 10-year average (NIFC), the EU is having its
second-largest season-to-date in 20 years (AFP/EFFIS) with the
Gironde/Landes complex driving our Mediterranean hotspot count up
every day this week, and the Northwest Territories boreal, at 3.9x
its seasonal average (CIFFC), is the largest fire complex on the
planet. The El Nino signal proper is still ahead: Indonesia's
hotspots are +167% on 2025 with the ministry explicitly citing El
Nino, but ASMC remains at Alert Level 1 with no transboundary haze,
and the Amazon's fire window (Sep-Jan) has not opened, though the Rio
Negro recession is tracking 2023, the record-drought year. None of
the northern-hemisphere anomalies should be attributed to El Nino;
the two R4-R5 regions this tracker exists for are still loading. The
first same-week analog pull confirms it from the detection side: the
Mediterranean control is at 1.8x its highest analog week while the
Amazon is running below all four recent analog years, and Indonesia
sits between its analogs, double 2023 but well under 2015.

## Where the fire is, globally (7 days, same sensor basis)

1. **Southern African savanna belt** (Angola, DRC, Zambia, Zimbabwe):
   ~297,000 detections across the seven largest cells. Routine
   dry-season agricultural burning, every year; context, not signal.
2. **Canadian boreal, Northwest Territories**: **145,196** detections,
   the largest coherent fire complex on the planet this week, centered
   on the Mackenzie basin cells. CIFFC (2026-07-24) has NWT at
   1,067,077 ha season-to-date, 3.9x its 10-year same-date average,
   most of it remote monitored-response fires; national total 3.37 M
   ha (18% above 10-yr average, but only ~28% of the record 2023
   pace). Note: BC is *below* average per CIFFC; the boreal burn is
   NWT plus Ontario (3.5x average). Canada is out of tracker scope
   with a weak ENSO link; the spec's re-inclusion trigger ("add
   Canada boreal only if a signal appears") has arguably fired.
   Decision for Kristjan.
3. **Northern Australia savanna**: 66,727. Normal July burning.
4. **Amazon box**: 27,307 (see table).
5. **US West box**: 26,691 (see table).
6. **Mediterranean box**: 24,748 (see table).

## Pilot caveats

- NRT product, not the science-quality archive; counts will be
  restated when baselines are built from the archive.
- 15-day window not shown this issue (the keyless NRT feeds cover 7
  days; the archive API now makes 15-day windows possible and they
  start with the first official issue).
- The FIRMS area API caps each request at a 5-day range; weekly pulls
  are chunked 5+2. Build detail for the fetcher.
- Low-confidence detections excluded; this choice must be mirrored
  exactly when baselines are built.
- EFFIS figures reached us via secondary reporting (AFP, The Corner,
  aggregators); the EFFIS statistics portal is a JS app that resisted
  direct reading. Getting a direct EFFIS pull is a to-do before the
  first official issue.
- Sub-national event figures (Gironde, La Mierla, Los Gallardos)
  moved fast this week and sources conflict on some of them; the
  weekly issue carries the dated figure and its source, not a
  reconciliation.
