# What is abnormal: European summer 2026, and the week to 2 September

Internal. Heat desk, 2026-09-04. Numbers measured 2026-09-04 (D-272).

Two separate questions, deliberately kept apart because they have
different evidence behind them and different confidence.

1. **The summer**, which is now closing and is exceptional.
2. **The last week**, which is a distinct and much thinner finding, and
   the one socials asked about.

---

## Read this first: coverage, because it bounds everything below

**Our set is 47 European cities, and that is not Europe.** We track
cities with century-scale daily records, which skews west and central.
Every count below ("26 of 47") is a property of that list. The per-city
margins are not: those stand on each station's own archive (D-141).

**The refresh reached three services of about ten.** French, Spanish and
Austrian sources moved today; German, Dutch, Swiss, UK and Nordic are
14 to 25 days stale. So:

    9 of 47 cities   season complete, numbers final
    38 of 47         near-final, and can only go UP
    3 Spanish cities returned no new data at all
    1 service returned an unparseable response

The stale cities are stale because of our pipeline, not because nothing
happened there. **Do not report their lower numbers as a cooler north.**

**A hot day is that station's own 95th percentile of July-August daily
maxima, 1971-2000 or 1991-2020.** Nottingham and Marseille are each
judged against themselves, so counts are comparable across cities in
kind but not in temperature.

---

## 1. The summer

### The headline is not the rank. It is the margin.

**26 of 47 cities sit at rank 1 on their own record, and not one is a
tie.** Every record was beaten outright. That is the unusual part: in a
normal record year a good share of the records are equalled.

    city          2026   prev    year    ratio
    Paris           38     17    2022    2.24x
    Nice            55     27    2003    2.04x
    Bordeaux        46     23    2025    2.00x
    Strasbourg      44     24    2022    1.83x
    London          35     20    2018    1.75x
    Frankfurt       32     20    2003    1.60x
    Marseille       50     33    2022    1.52x

**Paris more than doubled its previous record. Nice beat 2003 by 28
days**: the old record was a month of hot days and 2026 added another
month on top of it.

31 of 47 are in their own top three.

### The records being broken are long ones

    Vienna     hottest in 172 years   (series from 1855)
    Lugano     hottest in 162 years
    Zurich     hottest in 145 years
    Basel      hottest in 144 years

Basel's and Zurich's previous records were **1947**.

### 2003 is no longer the benchmark

**36 of 47 cities have already beaten their own 2003.** Among the
cities now at rank 1, the previous record was most often 2022 (7
cities) and 2003 (6), then 2025 (3).

The summer that killed on the order of 70,000 people across Europe is
now the second or third hottest on record at most of the cities we
track. **That is the comparison worth making, and it is a fact about
the record rather than a claim about mortality**, which we do not
measure.

### The pattern has a hard northern edge

Only three cities sit outside their own top quarter, and they are the
same place:

    Helsinki    48th percentile
    Tallinn     51st
    Stockholm   67th

Vilnius at the 88th is already inside the pattern. **This is not
Europe-wide warmth.** It is an intense western and central signal that
stops before the Nordic and Baltic north.

### Nights cannot carry this story

11 cities are at rank 1 on nights; **25 of 47 are gated** because a
night that never falls below 20 C is a Mediterranean measure and most
of our set has too few ever to rank. The days metric is doing the work
this summer. Do not reach for a nights number to support a northern
city.

## 2. The last week, to 2 September

**This is a much thinner finding than the summer and should be labelled
as such wherever it is used.**

**Coverage: eight French cities.** They are the only stations in our set
with September data. Spain stops at 31 August, Austria reaches 3
September, everything else is older. So this is a finding about France,
not about Europe, and specifically about Mediterranean France.

### The week itself is high but not remarkable

Mean daily maximum, 27 August to 2 September, ranked against the same
seven dates in every prior year:

    Toulouse      5 of 77      Nice          6 of 77
    Marseille     5 of 77      Montpellier   7 of 77
    Lyon          6 of 77      Strasbourg    8 of 77
    Paris        14 of 77      Bordeaux     26 of 77

Top ten for most, which is notable and is not a story on its own.

### The last two days are the story: it is intensifying, not fading

Mean daily maximum, 1 to 2 September, against the same two dates in
every prior year:

    Marseille      1 of 77    35.9 C   RECORD for the date
    Montpellier    2 of 77    35.8 C   (1st is 37.7 in 2016)
    Nice           4 of 77    33.0 C
    Toulouse       8 of 77
    Lyon           9 of 77
    Paris         13 of 77
    Strasbourg    14 of 77
    Bordeaux      19 of 77

**Marseille recorded its hottest 1-2 September in 77 years of record**,
beating 35.5 C in 1962.

The shape is the point: **the seven-day window ranks 5th and the final
two days rank 1st and 2nd.** The heat is building into September on the
Mediterranean coast while the Atlantic and northern French cities have
come off. Paris ranks 13th and 14th, and was at rank 1 for the summer.

### What this does NOT support

- **It is two days.** A two-day mean is a thin instrument and one more
  hot day would move these ranks materially.
- **We cannot see past 2 September.** If the wave continued, we do not
  know it. If it broke on the 3rd, we do not know that either.
- **It is not "Europe".** It is eight French stations, five of which
  are the ones that carry the signal.
- **Nothing here is attributed to El Nino.** The correlation work we
  have is for South America. This is a description of the record.

## For socials: what is safe to say

**Strongest, fully supported, from complete or near-final data:**

> Vienna has just had its hottest summer in 172 years of record.
> Paris had more than twice as many hot days as its previous record.
> Nice beat the 2003 benchmark by 28 days.

**Supported, and needs its qualifier attached:**

> 26 of the 47 European cities we track are at their hottest on record,
> and every one of those records was beaten rather than equalled.

The qualifier is "we track". Without it the sentence claims something
about Europe that we did not measure.

**The last-week angle, if used, needs both of these in the copy:**

> Marseille recorded its hottest 1-2 September in 77 years.

That is one city, two days, and true. It is a fair post. **What is not
fair is presenting it as a new European heatwave**, because we have
September data for eight cities in one country.

**Do not say:** anything comparing 2003's death toll to 2026, anything
about El Nino causing this, or any count framed as "X% of Europe".

## Data integrity note, because it affects anything pulled before today

**Any heat figure taken from our payload earlier today is suspect.** As
the northern seasons closed on 31 August, `effective_cut` treated a
FINISHED season as one that had NOT BEGUN and the emitter fell back to
reporting the previous season. Paris showed "2025, 15 hot days" while
2026's 38 sat in the series unmentioned. Sixteen cities were already in
that state.

The page would not have looked wrong: correct label, correct number for
that label, and the largest summer in the record simply absent. An
absence produced by the calendar and presented as the current state.

Fixed in `959ae6a6` and verified at six cut positions across both
hemispheres. **The live site still serves pre-refresh numbers** and is
behind on 47 cities until design re-renders.
